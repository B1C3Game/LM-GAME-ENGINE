# Onboarding Guide — Your First LM Game

Welcome to LM-ENGINE. You're about to play a game that runs in a chat interface. No app. No account. Just you, another person, and an LLM referee.

---

## The 5-Minute Setup

### Step 1: Choose a Game

Start here: [LM-ENGINE home](README.md)

Pick a game you want to try. For your first time, **Concept Drift** is the recommended entry point.

### Step 2: Read the Rules

Take 10 minutes to understand what you're doing. Read the game's README and RULES document.

**You don't need to memorize everything.** Just understand:
- What's the objective?
- How many rounds?
- How do I win or lose?

### Step 3: Find Another Player

Text or call someone and say: *"Want to play a quick game? It's like a conversation game, no download needed."*

They need to:
- Be online to see the chat (you can hand them the phone, or share the screen, or have them follow along on their own device)
- Be willing to play 15–30 minutes (depends on the game)
- Not have played this exact game before (fresh experience = more fun)

### Step 4: Copy the Master Prompt

Go to the game folder and find `GAME-MASTER-PROMPT.md`.

**Copy everything in that file.**

### Step 5: Paste Into Your LLM

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or your favorite LLM and create a new chat.

Paste the entire prompt into the chat box.

Send it.

The LLM will greet you and explain what's about to happen. Read that greeting carefully.

### Step 6: Identify Yourselves

The LLM will ask: *"Player 1 (or use your name): Are you ready?"*

Replace "Player 1" with your actual name or just say "Yes, I'm ready."

Both of you confirm. Then the game starts.

---

## During the Game

### The LLM Is the Referee

The LLM will:
- Explain what happens after your move
- Calculate your score
- Tell you whose turn it is
- Keep track of the game state

It's not your friend trying to make you feel good. It's a referee making fair calls.

### The Rules Are Law

If you're unsure whether your move is allowed, check the RULES document. The LLM has it memorized, but you should too.

If you think the LLM made a mistake, say so. The prompt includes an appeals process.

### The Conversation is the Game

You're not typing into a game board or menu. You're talking to the LLM like a person.

Example exchange:

> **LLM:** You have 30 seconds to think of a word that rhymes with "elephant." Go.
>
> **You:** Um... "telephant?" (It's not a real word, but it rhymes!)
>
> **LLM:** [Laughs] Okay, that technically rhymes, though you made up a word. +1 point. Player 2's turn.

## After the Game

### The Reveal

Most LM games end with a reveal—where the LLM shows you what you didn't know, or what you got wrong.

This is the best part. The reveal is often funny, surprising, or thought-provoking.

### Discuss What Happened

Spend 5 minutes talking about the game after it ends. Questions to ask each other:

- What surprised you?
- What made it hard?
- Did you understand each other?
- Would you play again?

### Should You Play Again?

You can! Most LM games are designed to be played multiple times. The outcomes will be different.

But new concepts, new players, or a fresh scenario might be more fun than repeating the same game.

## Troubleshooting

### The LLM Doesn't Understand a Rule

Copy the relevant rule from the RULES document and paste it again. Say: *"Re-read this rule, then tell me if my move is allowed."*

### I Disagree With a Score

Most games have an appeals process built into the prompt. The game will explain it. Use it.

If the LLM sticks to a decision you still disagree with, note it down for game balance feedback. (You can report bugs/balance issues [here](#reporting-issues).)

### One Player Can't Keep Up With the Pace

Ask the LLM to slow down. Between each turn, you can ask the LLM to:
- Summarize the current game state
- Explain a rule again
- Wait for you to think

The LLM will do this. Games should be fun, not stressful.

### The Game Doesn't Feel Right

If something is confusing or broken after 2–3 rounds, it's okay to stop and try a different game.

But before you give up, ask: *"Am I misunderstanding a rule?"* Check the RULES document again. Nine times out of ten, the game is fine and you just need a clarification.

## FAQs

### Do We Need to Be Online at the Same Time?

For the first game, yes. You need to see the LLM's responses as they come.

Later, if you're playing asynchronously (one person per day), the game state stays in the chat history. You can hand off the conversation like a notebook.

### Can I Play With Someone Over Text?

Yes. You can:
1. Share the chat link or screenshot each turn
2. Share the entire chat in a thread
3. Let one person control the conversation and send updates to the other

### Do I Need to Pay to Play?

Not if you already have access to an LLM (ChatGPT free tier, Claude, etc.). The games are free.

If you're paying for ChatGPT+ or Claude Pro, you're already paying. Games don't add extra cost.

### What If the LLM Runs Out of Context (Too Many Turns)?

Older LLM conversations can get long. If the conversation gets so long that the LLM forgets earlier rounds:

1. Copy the game state summary that the LLM just gave
2. Start a new chat
3. Paste the master prompt again
4. Tell it: *"Resume from [game state summary]. It's [Player X]'s turn."*

Most games finish in one chat thread (30–100 turns), so this rarely happens.

### Can I Play Solo?

No. LM games are designed for two people (or more, depending on the game). Playing both sides ruins the point—there's no miscommunication to discover.

## Philosophy: Why Play LM Games?

Normal video games have physics, graphics, and story. LM games have conversation.

They're slower, quieter, and weirder. They're not "better"—they're different.

What makes them special:

- **They expose miscommunication.** You discover what you didn't understand about each other.
- **They scale infinitely.** Any concept, any scenario, any ruleset. Just add a prompt.
- **They're collaborative.** You win or lose together. The LLM is the referee, not the adversary.
- **They work anywhere.** No installation. Works in ChatGPT, Claude, or any LLM. Works on phone or desktop.

Play one, and you'll see what we mean.
